# Azog's Goblin Warband — Amass Goblins Commander

Commander: Azog, Moria's Ruin (2 generic, Black — 3 CMC, mono-black)

Overview
- Strategy: Azog turns removal into an army — his ETB destroys a creature and hands its controller a growing Goblin Army token, and this deck stacks the deck so *you're* always the one amassing. Backed by a deep mono-black removal suite, a sacrifice/aristocrats subtheme (Deadly Dispute, Hungry Ghoul, Death Baron's Zombies/Skeletons), and drain effects that punish every death on the board, the game plan is simple: kill things, grow your Army, drain life, and close with oversized black beaters.
- Intended for: Both (single opponent and multiplayer pod)

## Lore

Azog strikes once, and Moria answers — every foe he fells swells his snarling Goblin Army. Bigger. Angrier. Never full. *One goblin's a nuisance. An Army's an extinction event.*

Key Synergies
- **Azog, Moria's Ruin + your own small creatures**: Point Azog's ETB at a 1-power creature or token you control to draw a card and immediately start (or grow) your own Goblin Army — turning "removal" into card advantage.
- **The Ozolith + Goblin/Orc Army tokens**: If a heavily-countered Army dies to a board wipe or removal spell, its counters land on The Ozolith instead of vanishing, ready to move onto your next threat.
- **Deadly Dispute / Hungry Ghoul + Reassembling Skeleton / Tunnel Rats**: Cheap, endlessly recurring sacrifice fodder feeds your sac outlets for card draw and stat growth without ever running dry.
- **Death Baron + Suspicious Shambler / Maalfeld Twins / Reassembling Skeleton**: Free +1/+1 and deathtouch turns your recursion-package chump blockers into real threats.
- **Vengeful Bloodwitch / Arnyn, Deathbloom Botanist + sacrifice outlets**: Every creature you sacrifice or trade in combat also drains an opponent — it adds up fast, especially in multiplayer.

Short mulligan and play notes
- Early game: Keep hands with 2-3 lands and cheap removal/ramp (Sol Ring, Mind Stone, Infernal Grasp, Fell). Play Azog around turn 3 into whatever's smallest on the opponents' board so you draw a card and start your Army immediately.
- Mid game: Chain removal into sacrifice outlets (Deadly Dispute, Hungry Ghoul, Stir Up Trouble) using Reassembling Skeleton/Tunnel Rats as renewable fodder. Recur creatures with Hazel's Nocturne, Pull from the Grave, or Cemetery Recruitment to keep bodies coming.
- Late game: Land Gathering of Darkness or a second Azog ETB to swing your Army into double digits, or just close with Kalakscion, Shredder, or Chitin Gravestalker while drain effects (Arnyn, Vengeful Bloodwitch, Ninja Teen) chip away at life totals.
- Key interactions: Save Gnashing of Teeth's sweep mode for a go-wide opponent; hold Pain 101/Undying-style protection for combat tricks on your best sac fodder; The Ozolith is your insurance policy if a board wipe threatens your Army.

Why these choices (summary)
- Real Goblin creatures are extremely scarce in this collection (only 3 total), so the deck leans into what Azog actually does mechanically — destroy-and-amass — rather than a traditional wide goblin swarm.
- Mono-black removal density (12 instants, 10 sorceries, many of them unconditional) ensures Azog always has a target and the deck can control the board in both 1v1 and multiplayer.
- Sacrifice/aristocrats support (Deadly Dispute, Hungry Ghoul, recurring fodder, death-trigger drainers) gives the deck a second, self-sustaining engine that doesn't depend on drawing Azog.
- Ramp is artifact-based (Sol Ring, Mind Stone, Prophetic Prism, etc.) since mono-black has no land ramp; this gets threats and removal online a turn or two early.

Analysis
**Strengths**:
- Extremely high removal density means the deck rarely runs out of answers, in either 1v1 or a crowded pod.
- The sacrifice/death-trigger package (Vengeful Bloodwitch, Arnyn, Ninja Teen, Death Baron) turns routine trades into steady incidental damage.
- Multiple recursion pieces (Hazel's Nocturne, Pull from the Grave, Zombify-style effects via Scrounge for Eternity) make the deck resilient to losing creatures.

**Weaknesses/Missing Staples**:
- True board wipes: the mono-black non-precon pool has no "destroy all creatures" sweeper — Gnashing of Teeth's one-player wipe is the closest thing. If you're willing to pull from Commander precons, a real wipe (e.g. Toxic Deluge, Damnation) would meaningfully improve the multiplayer matchup.
- Actual Goblin tribal payoffs: with only 3 Goblin creatures available, this is a "Goblin-flavored amass/aristocrats" deck rather than a classic wide Goblin swarm — acquiring more red Goblins (and a second color) would open up a true tribal build later.
- Card selection/tutoring: no way to find Azog or key pieces once buried in a big deck; a tutor effect would smooth out inconsistency.

Next steps (optional suggestions)
- If you're open to cannibalizing a Commander precon later, pulling in a real board wipe or two would shore up the multiplayer matchup significantly.
- Consider a light equipment package (Hammer of Nazahn, Sword of Vengeance) if you want to make Azog himself a recurring combat threat rather than a one-shot removal spell.
- Watch for future acquisitions of Goblin creatures — this collection is currently light on them, and a few more would let the deck lean harder into "amass" as a true go-wide plan.

## Suggested Deck Names

- **Amassive Attack** — A pun on the Army mechanic ("amass") and the growing, ever-bigger threat your Goblin Army becomes.
- **Moria's Reckoning** — Straight from the commander's flavor: Azog turning Moria's darkness into your engine of destruction.
- **One Goblin Army** — Literal and punchy: the whole deck compresses down to a single, ever-growing Army token.
- **Grudge Collector** — A double meaning: Azog "collects" kills (grudges) via removal, and the deck's recursion pieces literally collect creatures back from the graveyard.
