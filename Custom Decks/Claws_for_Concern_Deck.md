# Tovolar, Dire Overlord — Gruul Werewolf Tribal Commander

Commander: Tovolar, Dire Overlord // Tovolar, the Midnight Scourge (2RG, Red/Green)

## Overview
- **Strategy**: A werewolf/wolf tribal aggro deck. Cheap creatures flood the board and transform into bigger, scarier night-side threats; anthem effects (Immerwolf, Silverfur Partisan, Full Moon's Rise, Howlpack Resurgence) pump the whole team, and Tovolar rewards every werewolf/wolf connection with a card. Nightpack Ambusher and the daybound/nightbound werewolves push the game toward night, while transform-matters payoffs (Ascendant Packleader, Cult of the Waxing Moon, Runebound Wolf) turn each flip into value or reach. This revision doubles down on card advantage (Toski, Realmwalker, Shamanic Revelation), adds a genuine finisher for wide boards (Beastmaster Ascension), and patches the three gaps the previous draft flagged: flying defence (Cavalier of Thorns, Broken Wings), graveyard recursion (Cavalier of Thorns), and untapped mana (Arcane Signet, Ancient Ziggurat, Secluded Courtyard).
- **Intended for**: Both — the low curve (average CMC ~3.08, 24 cards at 1–2 mana) and cheap, targeted removal suit fast 1v1 games, while the wide Wolf token production, Toski's per-creature draw, Shamanic Revelation and Return of the Wildspeaker all scale as more opponents join a pod.
- **Source**: Iterated from the previous Claws for Concern draft using the full owned collection (`moxfield_latest.csv`). Every card below is owned.

## Lore

Under Tovolar's rule, the moon doesn't rise — it *recruits*. One howl and the pack goes from picket fence to pack of teeth. Gruul doesn't do subtle. It does *fang-tastic*.

## Decklist (100 cards)

### Commander (1)
- **Tovolar, Dire Overlord // Tovolar, the Midnight Scourge** — Draws a card whenever a Werewolf or Wolf you control deals combat damage to a player, and flips the game to night on your upkeep once you control three or more Wolves/Werewolves. The card-advantage and payoff engine for the whole tribal plan.

- **Breakneck Rider // Neck Breaker** — Old-style werewolf; night side gives all attacking creatures +1/+0 and trample.
- **Burly Breaker // Dire-Strain Demolisher** — A 6/5 ward {1} that becomes an 8/7 with ward {3} at night. The biggest, most removal-resistant body in the deck and a reliable trigger for Garruk's Uprising.

- **Geier Reach Bandit // Vildin-Pack Alpha** — Hasty by day; at night it transforms every Werewolf that enters, chaining Cult of the Waxing Moon and Silverfur Partisan triggers.

### Enchantments (7)
- **Beastmaster Ascension** *(new)* — Seven attack triggers turns on +5/+5 for the whole board. With Tovolar's Huntmaster minting two Wolves per attack at night and Nightpack Ambusher adding one per end step, this deck banks seven quest counters across two combats and then simply wins.
- **Full Moon's Rise** — Werewolves get +1/+0 and trample; sacrifice it to regenerate the whole pack through a wrath.
- **Garruk's Uprising** — Trample for the entire team plus a card every time a 4-plus power creature enters. Almost every night-side werewolf, Migloz, Cavalier of Thorns and every Return of the Wildspeaker turn triggers it.
- **Howlpack Resurgence** — Flash anthem: Wolves/Werewolves get +1/+1 and trample — a combat trick or a wrath-dodge.
- **Hunter's Talent** — Enters as a Rabid Bite, levels into an attack-trigger trample pump, then into "draw a card each end step if you control a 4-power creature." Removal, evasion and card advantage in one two-mana card.
- **Ranger Class** — Makes a Wolf on ETB, then counters on attackers at Level 2, then casts creatures off the top at Level 3.
- **Wolfwillow Haven** — Two-mana ramp that later cashes out for a 2/2 Wolf, so it's never a dead topdeck.

### Instants (9)

- **Snakeskin Veil** — Protects a key threat with hexproof and leaves a permanent +1/+1 counter behind.

## Key Synergies

- **Toski, Bearer of Secrets + Tovolar + a wide Wolf board**: Toski draws off *every* creature that connects, Tovolar draws off every Wolf/Werewolf that connects — so each Wolf token that gets through is two cards. Toski is indestructible and can't be countered, so the engine survives the sweeper that resets everything else.
- **Realmwalker + Tovolar's night trigger**: Realmwalker is a changeling, so it counts as a Wolf *and* a Werewolf. It fills the third body for Tovolar's upkeep flip, picks up Immerwolf's anthem, adds to Runebound Wolf's damage count, and casts werewolves off the top of your library at the same time.
- **Beastmaster Ascension + Tovolar's Huntmaster at night**: the night side makes two Wolves every attack, and Nightpack Ambusher adds one at every end step you hold up mana. Two combats with a wide board banks seven quest counters, and +5/+5 across eight-plus creatures ends the game on the spot.
- **Return of the Wildspeaker + a transformed board**: every night-side werewolf, every Wolf token, Migloz and Cavalier of Thorns are non-Human, so this is either "draw six or seven" off Burly Breaker/Wildblood Pack, or +3/+3 to eight-plus creatures at instant speed for a lethal swing out of nowhere.
- **Garruk's Uprising + Hunter's Talent + Migloz and Cavalier of Thorns**: Uprising gives the whole board trample and draws on every 4-plus power creature entering — Migloz is a 4/4 for three and Cavalier a 5/6 for five, so both replace themselves. Hunter's Talent level 3 adds a free card at every end step.
- **Oran-Rief, the Vastwood + Wolf tokens**: Huntmaster of the Fells, Tovolar's Huntmaster, Ranger Class, Nightpack Ambusher and the Arlinns all make *green* 2/2 Wolves; Oran-Rief puts a permanent +1/+1 counter on every one that entered that turn, turning a token swarm into a 3/3 army.
- **Cavalier of Thorns + Shamanic Revelation**: Cavalier blocks the fliers that used to race you unopposed, and when it finally dies it puts Shamanic Revelation (or Return of the Wildspeaker, or Tovolar himself) back on top of your library — the deck's first real recursion.

## Short Mulligan and Play Notes

- **Early game (turns 1–3)**: Sol Ring, Arcane Signet or Mind Stone on turn 1–2 into a cheap werewolf (Village Messenger, Ascendant Packleader, Scorned Villager); Wolfwillow Haven or Cultivate if the hand is land-light. Immerwolf, Duskwatch Recruiter, Realmwalker, Hunter's Talent, Beastmaster Ascension or Garruk's Uprising on turn 2–3. Keep hands with 3–4 lands and at least one one- or two-drop werewolf.
- **Mid game (turns 4–6)**: Land Tovolar behind Sol Ring/Air Shoes once three-plus Wolves/Werewolves are out so the night trigger comes online immediately. Toski turns every subsequent attack into cards. Deploy Nightpack Ambusher on an opponent's end step, then hold up Return of the Wildspeaker or Howlpack Resurgence to blow out combat.
- **Late game (turn 7+)**: Beastmaster Ascension at seven counters, or Return of the Wildspeaker in +3/+3 mode with Garruk's Uprising's trample, is frequently lethal out of nowhere. Shamanic Revelation refuels after a sweeper. Runebound Wolf and Kessig Wolf Run close games that combat can't, and Raging Ravine gives you a threat that survives a wrath.
- **Key interactions**: Immerwolf stops non-Human Werewolves from flipping back to day, so cast it before committing daybound creatures you want to keep on their bigger side. Count spells carefully each turn — casting exactly one keeps daybound creatures transforming to night, while two or more flips everything back to day. Nightpack Ambusher wants you to cast *nothing* on your own turn, which pairs naturally with holding up Return of the Wildspeaker, Broken Wings, Moonmist and Howlpack Resurgence. Remember Toski **must** attack each combat — plan around it rather than getting blown out by a fight spell.

## Why These Choices (Summary)

- **Card advantage was the cheapest upgrade available**: Toski, Realmwalker and Shamanic Revelation replaced a looter, a conditional 3/3 and a four-mana rummage. The deck now draws off attacking, off casting from the top of the library, and off simply having a board — three independent engines that don't stack a single dead card.
- **The three flagged weaknesses are addressed directly**: Cavalier of Thorns is reach *and* graveyard recursion in one body; Broken Wings kills the flier that was previously unanswerable; Arcane Signet, Ancient Ziggurat and Secluded Courtyard cut the tapped-land count from nine to seven while adding a colour-perfect rock.
- **Low, aggressive curve preserved**: the swaps are close to cost-neutral (average CMC moved 3.05 → 3.08) and every new card either replaces itself or ends the game.
- **Removal stayed targeted, deliberately**: with 36 creatures, symmetric sweepers (Blasphemous Act, Chain Reaction) cost more than they gain here. Decimate was the one piece of removal that could be *uncastable*; Migloz gives repeatable artifact/enchantment answers on a body instead.
- **Every card is owned**: verified against `moxfield_latest.csv`.

## Analysis

**Strengths**:
- Three independent draw engines (Tovolar, Toski, Garruk's Uprising) plus Realmwalker, Duskwatch Recruiter, Werewolf Pack Leader and Hunter's Talent — the deck almost never runs out of gas
- Toski and Raging Ravine both survive board wipes, so the deck has a rebuild plan even without recursion
- Two genuine "win from nowhere" buttons: Return of the Wildspeaker at instant speed, and Beastmaster Ascension once it's online
- Multiple layers of anthem effects mean the board keeps getting bigger even after a token sweep
- Low curve and flash/instant-speed threats let it play well on either player's turn

**Weaknesses/Missing Staples**:
- **Board wipes**: still no sweeper. This remains a conscious trade — the collection's Gruul options (Blasphemous Act, Chain Reaction) are symmetric and would kill more of your board than theirs
- **Graveyard recursion**: improved but thin — Cavalier of Thorns is the only piece, and it returns the card to the top of your library rather than your hand
- **Flying defence**: better, not solved. Cavalier of Thorns, Daybreak Ranger and Broken Wings are the whole package against an evasive deck
- **Counterspells / stack interaction**: none, as is normal for Gruul — a combo opponent has to be raced or removed at sorcery speed
- **Tapped lands**: down to seven of the twelve non-basics, but Ancient Ziggurat's creature-only restriction means a small number of awkward turns where you can't cast a noncreature spell

- Test whether Immerwolf's "can't transform back to day" clause ever costs value against Cult of the Waxing Moon and Geier Reach Bandit triggers that want repeated transforms
- Cavalier of Thorns is {2}{G}{G}{G} — if the triple green proves awkward, Greenwarden of Murasa (owned) is straight recursion at {4}{G}{G} with no reach
- Mild-Mannered Librarian and Wargling (both owned) are the only other Wolf/Werewolf-relevant bodies in the collection if you want a higher tribal count for Runebound Wolf
- Kindred Summons (owned, {5}{G}{G} instant) is a genuine haymaker with a wide board — worth testing over Sage of Ancient Lore in pod-heavy play
- Lightning Greaves (owned) can join or replace Air Shoes if you want a zero-cost equip, at the price of shroud stopping your own pump spells
- If the collection expands, a one-sided sweeper or Rhythm of the Wild would round out the remaining gaps
