# Tovolar, Dire Overlord — Gruul Werewolf Tribal Commander

Commander: Tovolar, Dire Overlord // Tovolar, the Midnight Scourge (2RG, Red/Green)

## Overview
- **Strategy**: A werewolf/wolf tribal aggro deck. Cheap creatures flood the board and transform into bigger, scarier night-side threats; anthem effects (Immerwolf, Silverfur Partisan, Full Moon's Rise, Howlpack Resurgence) pump the whole team, and Tovolar rewards every werewolf/wolf connection with a card. Nightpack Ambusher and the newer daybound/nightbound werewolves push the game toward night, while transform-matters payoffs (Ascendant Packleader, Cult of the Waxing Moon, Runebound Wolf) turn each flip into value or reach. This build adds a proper trample-and-draw engine (Garruk's Uprising, Hunter's Talent), a mass pump/refuel button (Return of the Wildspeaker), real ramp (Cultivate, Wolfwillow Haven) and commander protection (Air Shoes).
- **Intended for**: Both — the low curve (average CMC ~3.05, 26 cards at 1–2 mana) and cheap, targeted removal suit fast 1v1 games, while the wide Wolf token production, Return of the Wildspeaker and Tovolar's per-hit card draw keep scaling as more opponents join a pod.
- **Source**: Upgraded from the user-supplied werewolf list using the full owned collection (`moxfield_latest.csv`). Six cards in the original list were not in the collection or were format-weak; every card below is owned.

## Lore

Under Tovolar's rule, the moon doesn't rise — it *recruits*. One howl and the pack goes from picket fence to pack of teeth. Gruul doesn't do subtle. It does *fang-tastic*.

## Decklist (100 cards)

### Commander (1)
- **Tovolar, Dire Overlord // Tovolar, the Midnight Scourge** — Draws a card whenever a Werewolf or Wolf you control deals combat damage to a player, and flips the game to night on your upkeep once you control three or more Wolves/Werewolves. The card-advantage and payoff engine for the whole tribal plan.

## Key Synergies

- **Tovolar, Dire Overlord + Immerwolf + Silverfur Partisan**: Every Wolf/Werewolf connection draws a card off Tovolar; Immerwolf pumps the team and locks non-Human Werewolves at their bigger night-side stats, while Silverfur Partisan mints a Wolf every time one of your fight spells targets your own creature.
- **Return of the Wildspeaker + a transformed board**: Every night-side werewolf and every Wolf token is a non-Human, so this is either "draw six or seven" off Burly Breaker/Wildblood Pack, or +3/+3 to eight-plus creatures at instant speed for a lethal swing out of nowhere.
- **Garruk's Uprising + Hunter's Talent + big night sides**: Uprising gives the whole board trample and draws on every 4-plus power creature entering (Burly Breaker, Tovolar's Huntmaster, Wildsong Howler, Village Reavers); Hunter's Talent level 3 adds a free card at every end step. Two cheap enchantments turn the aggro board into a draw engine.
- **Into the Night / Unnatural Moonrise + daybound werewolves**: Both force night immediately, flipping every daybound creature (Instigator Gang, Kruin Outlaw, Sage of Ancient Lore) to its stronger side a turn early, while replacing themselves with a loot or a card.
- **Oran-Rief, the Vastwood + Wolf tokens**: Huntmaster of the Fells, Tovolar's Huntmaster, Ranger Class, Nightpack Ambusher and the Arlinns all make *green* 2/2 Wolves; Oran-Rief puts a permanent +1/+1 counter on every one that entered that turn, turning a token swarm into a 3/3 army.
- **Air Shoes (Swiftfoot Boots) + Tovolar**: Haste means Tovolar attacks and starts drawing the turn he lands; hexproof means the removal spell your opponent held up does nothing.

## Short Mulligan and Play Notes

- **Early game (turns 1–3)**: Sol Ring or Mind Stone on turn 1–2 into a cheap werewolf (Village Messenger, Ascendant Packleader, Scorned Villager); Wolfwillow Haven or Cultivate on two/three if the hand is land-light. Immerwolf, Duskwatch Recruiter, Hunter's Talent or Garruk's Uprising on turn 2–3. Keep hands with 3–4 lands and at least one one- or two-drop werewolf.
- **Mid game (turns 4–6)**: Land Tovolar behind Sol Ring/Air Shoes once three-plus Wolves/Werewolves are out so the night trigger comes online immediately. Deploy Nightpack Ambusher on an opponent's end step, then hold up Return of the Wildspeaker or Howlpack Resurgence to blow out combat.
- **Late game (turn 7+)**: Return of the Wildspeaker in +3/+3 mode with Garruk's Uprising's trample is frequently lethal out of nowhere. Runebound Wolf and Kessig Wolf Run close games that combat can't, and Raging Ravine gives you a threat that survives a sweeper.
- **Key interactions**: Immerwolf stops non-Human Werewolves from flipping back to day, so cast it before committing daybound creatures you want to keep on their bigger side. Count spells carefully each turn — casting exactly one keeps daybound creatures transforming to night, while two or more flips everything back to day. Nightpack Ambusher wants you to cast *nothing* on your own turn, which pairs naturally with holding up Return of the Wildspeaker, Moonmist and Howlpack Resurgence.

## Why These Choices (Summary)

- **Low, aggressive curve preserved**: 26 of 63 non-land cards still cost 1–2 mana. The upgrades went into two-mana enchantments (Hunter's Talent, Wolfwillow Haven, Air Shoes) rather than more top end.
- **Anthem-heavy tribal shell**: Immerwolf, Silverfur Partisan, Full Moon's Rise and Howlpack Resurgence all pump the same Wolf/Werewolf base that Tovolar and Runebound Wolf already care about, so every card pulls double duty.
- **Trample was the missing multiplier**: a board of 4/4s and 5/5s gets chump-blocked to death. Garruk's Uprising, Hunter's Talent level 2 and Untamed Pup give the whole team trample and turn Return of the Wildspeaker into a finisher.
- **Removal stays targeted, deliberately**: with 36 creatures, symmetric sweepers (Blasphemous Act, Chain Reaction) cost more than they gain here, so the deck answers threats one at a time with Abrade, Bite Down, Chaos Warp, Moonlight Hunt, Wolf Strike, Hunter's Talent and Decimate.
- **Every card is owned**: four lands in the original list (Gruul Guildgate, Gruul Turf, Mossfire Valley, Omashu City) are not in `moxfield_latest.csv`; they are replaced with owned Gruul lands.

## Analysis

**Strengths**:
- Multiple layers of anthem effects mean the board keeps getting bigger even after a token sweep
- Card draw is baked into the plan via Tovolar, Garruk's Uprising, Hunter's Talent, Duskwatch Recruiter, Werewolf Pack Leader and Wolfkin Outcast — the deck rarely runs out of gas
- Return of the Wildspeaker is a genuine "win from nowhere" button: instant speed, no board commitment, and the deck's creatures are almost all non-Human at night
- Low curve and flash/instant-speed threats (Nightpack Ambusher, Howlpack Resurgence) let it play well on either player's turn
- Runebound Wolf, Kessig Wolf Run and Raging Ravine provide finishers that don't depend on Tovolar surviving

**Weaknesses/Missing Staples**:
- **Board wipes**: still no sweeper. This is a conscious trade — the collection's Gruul options (Blasphemous Act, Chain Reaction, Fuel the Flames) are symmetric and would kill more of your board than theirs. Decimate is the closest substitute
- **Graveyard recursion**: no way to rebuy a key werewolf or planeswalker. Greenwarden of Murasa and Elvish Regrower are owned but cost 6 and 4 for effects this curve can't afford
- **Flying defence**: outside Daybreak Ranger, the deck has almost no answer to evasive fliers — it races rather than blocks
- **Ramp is better but not deep**: Sol Ring, Mind Stone, The Celestus, Cultivate, Rampant Growth, Wolfwillow Haven and three mana-dork werewolves. Colour-screwed hands can still stumble
- **Tapped lands**: seven of the twelve non-basics enter tapped, which occasionally costs a turn on an aggressive curve

## Next Steps (Optional Suggestions)

- Test whether Immerwolf's "can't transform back to day" clause ever costs value against Cult of the Waxing Moon and Geier Reach Bandit triggers that want repeated transforms
- If pod games run long, Shamanic Revelation (owned) is a strong alternative refuel to Thrill of Possibility
- Mild-Mannered Librarian and Wargling (both owned) are the only other Wolf/Werewolf-relevant bodies in the collection if you want a higher tribal count for Runebound Wolf
- Lightning Greaves (owned) can join or replace Air Shoes if you want a zero-cost equip, at the price of shroud stopping your own pump spells
- If the collection expands, a one-sided sweeper (e.g. Hour of Devastation effects) or Rhythm of the Wild would round out the two real gaps
