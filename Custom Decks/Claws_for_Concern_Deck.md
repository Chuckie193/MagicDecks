# Claws for Concern — Tovolar Gruul Werewolf Tribal Commander

Commander: Tovolar, Dire Overlord // Tovolar, the Midnight Scourge (1RG, Red/Green)

## Overview
- **Strategy**: Werewolf/Wolf tribal aggro. Cheap daybound creatures flood the board and flip into far larger night-side threats; anthems (Immerwolf, Silverfur Partisan, Full Moon's Rise, Howlpack Resurgence) lift the whole team, and Tovolar turns every connection into a card and pushes the game to night once three Wolves are out. Cult of the Waxing Moon turns each flip into an extra body, while Runebound Wolf and Ascendant Packleader convert a wide board into reach and counters. This upgrade adds the two things the original list lacked outright — a real draw engine (Garruk's Uprising, Return of the Wildspeaker) and enough ramp to deploy on curve (Cultivate, Arcane Signet, Wolfwillow Haven) — plus commander protection (Air Shoes) and a single reset button (Blasphemous Act).
- **Intended for**: Both — the low curve (average CMC 3.11, 27 cards at 1–2 mana) and cheap targeted removal suit fast 1v1 games, while wide Wolf token production, Return of the Wildspeaker and Tovolar's per-hit draw keep scaling as more opponents join a pod.
- **Source**: Upgraded from **The Bark Ages** Commander precon (`Precons/Commander Precons/TheBarkAges.txt`) using the full owned collection (`moxfield_latest.csv`). Every card below is owned; 91 of the original 100 cards were retained.

## Lore

Dusk falls on Kessig and the neighbours stop being neighbourly. Tovolar doesn't recruit a pack — he *reminds* one what it always was. By moonrise the village is his, fang and claw. Resistance is *fur-tile*.

## Decklist (100 cards)

### Commander (1)
- **Tovolar, Dire Overlord // Tovolar, the Midnight Scourge** — Draws a card whenever a Werewolf or Wolf you control deals combat damage to a player, and flips the game to night on your upkeep once you control three or more of them. The card-advantage engine and the night-switch the whole deck is built around.

## Key Synergies

- **Tovolar, Dire Overlord + any three Wolves**: Tovolar flips the game to night on your upkeep once you control three Wolves/Werewolves, transforming every daybound creature at once. Wolf tokens count, so Ranger Class, Wolfwillow Haven and Arlinn all help force night on your schedule rather than the opponent's.
- **Garruk's Uprising + Burly Breaker / Sage of Ancient Lore**: Uprising draws on entry with a power-4 creature out and draws again for each one that follows — and most night-side werewolves are power 4 or greater. It also grants the whole team trample, which turns Wolf tokens from chump-blocked chaff into real damage.
- **Return of the Wildspeaker + transformed werewolves**: Every flipped werewolf is a non-Human, so Return either draws cards equal to your biggest night-side body (often 5–8) or pumps the entire pack +3/+3 at instant speed for a surprise lethal swing.
- **Silverfur Partisan + Bite Down / Moonlight Hunt / Snakeskin Veil**: Each of these targets your own Wolf or Werewolf, so every removal spell or protection trick also mints a free 2/2 Wolf — which in turn pushes you toward Tovolar's night threshold.
- **Geier Reach Bandit (night side) + Cult of the Waxing Moon**: Vildin-Pack Alpha transforms each Werewolf as it enters, and Cult makes a 2/2 Wolf every time a permanent transforms into a non-Human — so each new werewolf arrives with a Wolf attached.
- **Immerwolf + the daybound team**: Immerwolf stops non-Human Werewolves transforming back to day, so once you flip to night the team stays on its larger side even when an opponent casts a spell on their turn.
- **Kessig Wolf Run + Air Shoes**: Boots make Tovolar hexproof and hasty, and Wolf Run dumps every spare mana into him as +X/+0 with trample — a protected, evasive finisher that closes 1v1 games quickly.

## Short mulligan and play notes

- **Early game (turns 1–3)**: Keep hands with two lands and a one- or two-drop. Village Messenger, Ascendant Packleader and Mild-Mannered Librarian all start the clock and, just as importantly, start counting toward Tovolar's three-creature night threshold. Scorned Villager and Weaver of Blossoms into a turn-three Tovolar is the ideal curve.
- **Mid game (turns 4–7)**: Get to three Wolves/Werewolves and let Tovolar flip the game to night on your upkeep — that single trigger can transform four or five creatures at once. Land Garruk's Uprising before committing further so the extra bodies replace themselves and gain trample.
- **Late game (turns 8+)**: Kessig Wolf Run turns a stalled board into lethal, Return of the Wildspeaker either refuels or delivers a +3/+3 alpha strike, and Runebound Wolf closes games that combat cannot.
- **Key interactions**: Remember that casting a spell on an opponent's turn keeps it day — Moonmist, Unnatural Moonrise and The Celestus let you flip to night regardless. Nightpack Ambusher rewards you for *not* casting on your own turn, so hold it and your instants up together. Blasphemous Act is deliberately your only sweeper: cast it when you are behind, never when your own board is the widest.

## Why these choices (summary)

- **The original list's real gap was card flow, not threats.** Thirty-six creatures is plenty; what it lacked was a way to refill. Garruk's Uprising and Return of the Wildspeaker both key off power-4 and non-Human creatures, which the night sides supply automatically, so the draw is free rather than a tax on the theme.
- **Ramp went from six sources to eight, weighted toward two-mana plays.** Arcane Signet, Cultivate and Wolfwillow Haven fix the heavy double-pip costs and make a turn-three Tovolar routine. Wolfwillow Haven was chosen over a generic rock specifically because it converts into a Wolf later, so it is never a dead topdeck.
- **Combat tricks were the weakest slots.** Horrid Vigor, Uncaged Fury and Wolf Strike each cost a card for a one-turn effect that a removal spell answers for free. Their slots went to permanents that keep generating value.
- **Exactly one sweeper, by design.** The skill guidance suggests 2–4 board wipes for a deck meant for both 1v1 and pods, but this deck wins *through* its own board. Blasphemous Act is included because its cost scales down to one or two mana on a clogged table, making it a genuine catch-up button; a second or third wipe would fight the gameplan more than it would help.
- **Two lands cut, none added.** Temple of the False God and Study Hall were the only lands that could not reliably cast a one- or two-drop. At 35 lands with eight ramp sources and an average cost of 3.11, the mana is more consistent than it was at 37.

## Analysis

**Strengths**:
- Extremely low curve for a tribal deck — 27 of 65 nonland cards cost one or two mana, so the board is established before most decks have stabilised.
- Night-side stats are far above rate; a flipped board is often 30+ power across seven or eight bodies, and Garruk's Uprising makes all of it trample.
- Redundant anthems (Immerwolf, Howlpack Resurgence, Full Moon's Rise, Mayor of Avabruck, Kessig Naturalist) mean removing any one lord barely slows the deck.
- Tovolar replaces himself with cards on every connection, so the aggression is not the all-in kind that runs out of gas.

**Weaknesses/Missing Staples**:
- **Board wipes**: one, by choice. Against a deck that goes wider or bigger than you, Blasphemous Act is the only true reset, and it kills your own board too.
- **Graveyard recursion**: none in Gruul-identity in the collection worth a slot — once a threat is dealt with it stays dealt with.
- **Flying / interaction with fliers**: Daybreak Ranger's ping is the only dedicated answer to an evasive threat; the deck fundamentally races on the ground.
- **Counterplay to the day/night switch**: opponents casting spells on their own turn can hold the game on day. The deck carries The Celestus, Moonmist and Unnatural Moonrise as forced flips, but a patient opponent can slow the transform plan down.
- **Targeted enchantment removal**: Chaos Warp, Decimate and Ruinous Intrusion cover it, but none is cheap.

## Next steps (optional suggestions)

- Playtest the single-sweeper choice. If pods regularly go wider than you, Bane of Progress is already in the collection and hits the artifact/enchantment decks Gruul struggles with.
- Toski, Bearer of Secrets is owned and in-identity: it is uncounterable, indestructible, draws on every connection and is non-Human for Return of the Wildspeaker — the strongest card considered but cut for being off-tribe.
- If you acquire more werewolf-specific lords or a second copy-effect for Wolf tokens, the anthem count supports going wider still.
- Consider swapping a tapped Gruul dual (Savage Mansion or Gongaga, Reactor Town) for an untapped one if you pick one up — the curve punishes tapped lands on turns one and two.

## Suggested Deck Names

- **Claws for Concern** — the current name, and still the best fit: a werewolf pun that also describes exactly what your opponents feel when the board flips at once.
- **Night Shift** — punchy two-worder; the deck's entire plan is clocking on when it turns night.
- **Howl of a Time** — "hell of a time" with the tribe's signature noise, and a nod to how quickly games end once Tovolar connects.
- **Moonlighting** — double meaning: working a second job by night, and the whole team getting better after dark.
