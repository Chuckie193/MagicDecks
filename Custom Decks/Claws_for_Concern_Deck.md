# Claws for Concern — Gruul Werewolf Tribal Commander

Commander: Tovolar, Dire Overlord // Tovolar, the Midnight Scourge (1RG, Red/Green)

## Overview
- **Strategy**: Tovolar werewolf tribal, upgraded with **18 purchased cards**. Cheap werewolves flood the board, Tovolar's upkeep trigger flips the team to night, and Garruk's Uprising gives everything trample while drawing off 4-power creatures entering. Hollowhenge Overlord multiplies the tribal board every upkeep; Zopandrel and Unnatural Growth double power and toughness at every combat.
- **Intended for**: Both — a low curve (average CMC ~3.27, 20 cards at 1–2 mana) and 11 pieces of interaction for 1v1, while Hollowhenge Overlord's token engine and Avabruck Caretaker's team hexproof scale into a pod.
- **Card pool**: Everything owned **plus 18 purchases**, all **under €15 each**. Nothing is dismantled — cards locked in an assembled deck are bought as second copies.

## Lore

The pack was always this big. It just never had the teeth to prove it. Now the woods hold their breath, the moon does as it's told, and something very old shakes off a very long sleep. *Consider this your notice.*

## Key Synergies

- **Garruk's Uprising + any two anthems = every Wolf token draws a card.** This is the deck's strongest interaction and the reason Garruk's is better than "13 four-power creatures" suggests. Garruk's checks power **as a creature enters, with anthems already applied**, and the deck runs five +1/+1 anthems for Wolves — Immerwolf, Howlpack Alpha, Nightpack Ambusher, Lord of the Ulvenwald and Howlpack Resurgence. With any two out, a 2/2 Wolf token **enters as a 4/4 and draws you a card.** Hollowhenge Overlord making six to nine tokens at your upkeep then draws six to nine cards.
- **Hollowhenge Overlord + a developed board**: a 2/2 Wolf for **each Wolf or Werewolf you control**, every upkeep, and those tokens count themselves next turn. Flash dodges sorcery-speed removal.
- **Avabruck Caretaker + Immerwolf**: flip the Caretaker once and Immerwolf locks it on its night side, so **all your other permanents have hexproof for the rest of the game** — protecting Tovolar, Unnatural Growth and Garruk's Uprising. **This is mutually exclusive with repeat value from Ulrich, Huntmaster, Vildin-Pack Alpha and Daybreak Ranger**, which all want to keep flipping. Choose one plan per game.
- **Zopandrel + Unnatural Growth**: both double at the beginning of each combat and compound, so a 2/2 Wolf attacks as an 8/8 — or 16/16 carrying two anthems. They fail to *different* answers — creature removal versus enchantment removal. Both trigger **before attackers are declared**, so respond to the trigger with Kessig Wolf Run or Tovolar's `{X}{R}{G}` and the pump gets doubled too; the doubling locks in each creature's power as the ability resolves.
- **Rhythm of the Wild + the top end**: riot means Hollowhenge Overlord, Avabruck Caretaker, Ulrich and every other five- and six-drop attacks the turn it lands, which is what pays for the higher curve.
- **Master of the Wild Hunt + true Wolves**: the activation taps **Wolves only**, so its ammunition is the nine true Wolves, Realmwalker and every token — which is exactly what Hollowhenge Overlord, Ranger Class and Nightpack Ambusher keep producing. Remember the target shoots back.
- **Immerwolf + The Celestus — a two-way payoff, not just a trade-off**: The Celestus loots whenever the designation *changes*, and the flip **back to day** is what powers it — which happens whenever any player casts two spells in their own turn, including you. Normally that flip would strip your night faces. Immerwolf stops non-Human Werewolves transforming, so your flipped bodies **stay flipped while day and night keep oscillating underneath them** — you keep the night stats *and* keep collecting the loot, roughly twice a turn cycle. Tovolar's own night face and Hollowhenge Huntmaster are both non-Human Werewolves and so both protected; Arlinn, the Moon's Fury is a planeswalker and will still flip back.
- **Growing Rites of Itlimoc**: flips with four creatures, usually by turn three or four, and then taps for `{G}` per creature — what finally makes `{G}{G}{G}{G}` on Unnatural Growth routine.

## Short Mulligan and Play Notes

- **Mulligan to *deploy and protect* Tovolar, not to find him.** He's always in the command zone. You want mana to cast him turn 3–4, two or three cheap bodies for his threshold, and ideally Air Shoes.
- **Early (1–3)**: Llanowar Elves or Sol Ring into a three-drop — there are twenty-one. Garruk's Uprising or Growing Rites on turn three are the best non-Tovolar starts.
- **Mid (4–6)**: Avabruck Caretaker is the highest-value six-drop. Hollowhenge Overlord starts compounding from the upkeep after it lands.
- **Late (7+)**: Either doubler ends the game on a wide board.
- **The Immerwolf decision is the deck's biggest piloting call.** It locks your night-side stats in — but it permanently switches off **four** flip-back effects: Huntmaster's Wolf-and-life, Ulrich's fight, Vildin-Pack Alpha's, and Daybreak Ranger's. Play it when you want the board frozen at its best; hold it while those cards are generating value.
- **Don't put +1/+1 counters on Pyreheart Wolf.** Wilderland Scrounger, Ranger Class L2, Hound Tamer, Packsong Pup and Arlinn's +1 all permanently disable its undying. Riot on Rhythm of the Wild should be **haste** for it, not a counter.
- **The Celestus is a toggle.** If it's already night, activating it turns it back to **day**. It also makes it day on entry if it's neither.
- **Realmwalker's naming choice is real**: Wolf gets you Hollowhenge Overlord and Pyreheart Wolf off the top; Werewolf gets you the 28-card half.
- **Day/night, stated correctly**: it becomes night when a player casts **zero** spells during their own turn; one spell changes nothing; two or more flips it back to day. **Tovolar's upkeep trigger is your real night button.**
- **Key rules**: daybound permanents can't be transformed by any effect, so Moonmist and Vildin-Pack Alpha only reach the 12 old-border werewolves. A werewolf **transforming is not entering**, so Garruk's Uprising draws off creatures you *cast*.

## Analysis

**Strengths**
- **Garruk's Uprising plus two anthems turns every Wolf token into a card.** With Hollowhenge Overlord that is six to nine cards an upkeep — the best thing the deck does.
- Two token engines that compound with the tribal count rather than needing setup
- Team hexproof from Avabruck Caretaker, locked permanently by Immerwolf
- Two P/T doublers that fail to different answers, plus permanent team trample
- **38 tribal bodies** — above The Bark Ages precon's 35 and the online field average of 29
- Mana rebalanced to the actual pip demand; Rhythm of the Wild makes the top end immediate

**Weaknesses**
- **The night plan is still commander-dependent, and this is unbuyable.** Only four cards in Magic can make it night; you own all four and this deck plays two of them.
- **Heroic Intervention covers one class of wipe — destruction.** It does **not** save you from Toxic Deluge (−X/−X beats indestructible), Cyclonic Rift or Farewell (untargeted beats hexproof), or edicts. Rhythm of the Wild's uncounterable clause protects threats on the way in only, never once they've resolved. With 44 creatures and no counterspells, **a resolved non-destroy sweeper still ends the game. That is the deck's ceiling and no purchase under €15 changes it.**
- **Zero counterspells, discard or graveyard hate.** An instant-speed combo cannot be interacted with.
- **Zero fliers and one reach body.** A resilient evasive threat has to be answered with a card, not a plan.
- **Twenty-one of 63 nonland cards cost exactly three, so the first four turns look the same most games** — play a werewolf, play a werewolf, attack. Rhythm of the Wild helps (haste makes the top of the curve live on arrival, and riot is a real per-creature choice) but does not solve it. The genuine decision points arrive later: flash timing on Nightpack Ambusher and Hollowhenge Overlord, whether to hold spells to keep it night, trigger ordering at the beginning of combat, the Immerwolf call, and Realmwalker naming Wolf vs Werewolf. That is the honest cost of 38 tribal bodies
- Interaction is 11 cards but only two (Beast Within, Chaos Warp) are unconditional; most of the rest need a board, so they're worst when you're behind

**Power level**: **Bracket 3, low-to-mid.** It beats low bracket 3 decks without becoming one. It loses to non-destroy wipes, instant-speed combo, and a removal spell on Tovolar before Avabruck Caretaker is online.
