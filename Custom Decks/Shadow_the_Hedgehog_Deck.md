# Shadow the Hedgehog — Rakdos Haste Aristocrats Commander

Commander: Shadow the Hedgehog ({B}{B}{R}{R}, Black/Red)

## Overview
- **Strategy**: Shadow turns speed into cards. Every creature you control **with haste or flash** draws you a card when it dies, so the deck is built to make cheap bodies hasty, throw them at the opponent, and then sacrifice them for profit. Four equipment and a repeatable haste-granter mean even a Reassembling Skeleton counts; free sacrifice outlets (Woe Strider, Immersturm Predator) convert every attacker into a card at end of combat. **Chaos Control** is the second axis: any spell cast with *at least one mana from an artifact* gains **split second**, so once a rock or a Treasure is online, your removal and your lethal swing cannot be answered. Nine Treasure producers and seven mana rocks keep that switch permanently flipped.
- **Intended for**: **Single opponent (1v1 / duel)** — 23 two-drops, a 3.0-ish average curve, twelve pieces of targeted removal, exactly one sweeper, and a commander who replaces every creature the opponent kills.
- **Card pool**: **Full collection** (`moxfield_latest.csv`), no reserved-deck or precon subtraction applied. Note that this borrows **Smaug the Impenetrable** from the in-progress *Hoard Mentality* draft and **Sol Ring / Command Tower / Arcane Signet** from precons — that is what "full collection" permits, but it does mean physical cannibalisation.

## Lore

Black streaks across the ruin, rings scattering. His crew charges in — and blinks out mid-stride. Time stops. Shadow walks the frozen world alone, gathering what they left behind. *You're too slow. You always were.*

## Key Synergies
- **Shadow the Hedgehog + Power Sneakers (Lightning Greaves)**: equip costs {0}, so you can move haste onto any creature at instant speed — including onto a creature that is about to die to removal or a sacrifice outlet. This is how the deck makes *every* body Shadow-eligible, not just the hasty ones.
- **Shadow the Hedgehog + Woe Strider / Immersturm Predator**: free sacrifice outlets mean a hasty attacker that gets blocked or targeted can always be cashed in for a card first. Combined with Vampiric Rites or Midnight Reaper, one sacrificed creature draws two.
- **Sol Ring (or any Treasure) + Chaos Control**: spend one artifact mana toward a spell and it gains **split second** — the opponent cannot respond, cannot counter, cannot sacrifice in response to your Infernal Grasp, cannot flash in a blocker. Nine Treasure producers and seven rocks make this the default state, not a corner case.
- **Skullclamp + Reassembling Skeleton**: {1}{B} to return the Skeleton, {1} to equip, and the -1 toughness kills it for **two cards**. Repeatable every turn, and with Greaves attached it draws a third off Shadow.
- **Determined Iteration + any creature token**: each combat you get a free hasty copy that dies at end of turn — a permanent, no-cost source of both extra damage and an extra Shadow draw.
- **Riveteers Requisitioner (blitz) + Shadow**: blitz gives it haste and "when this dies, draw a card," and it dies on its own at end of turn. That is a card from blitz, a card from Shadow, and a Treasure from its own death trigger, for three mana.

## Short Mulligan and Play Notes
- **Keep**: two lands plus a rock, or three lands with a two-drop. Mulligan hands with no black source — the commander needs {B}{B}, and eleven Swamps plus nine duals is enough that you will usually find one.
- **Early game (turns 1–3)**: lead on a rock, then a cheap body. Fanatical Firebrand, Vengeful Bloodwitch, Zulaport Cutthroat and Riveteers Requisitioner all attack or trade profitably. Do not hold removal for something better in 1v1 — kill the first real threat. If you have artifact mana, remember every removal spell you cast is now **split second**.
- **Mid game (turns 4–6)**: land Shadow, then keep a haste source in play. Reckless Stormseeker or Power Sneakers turns the draw trigger on permanently. From here, the loop is: attack with everything, sacrifice whatever gets blocked or targeted, draw two or three cards a turn, and redeploy.
- **Late game (turns 7+)**: Goldspan Dragon, Smaug the Magnificent or Smaug the Impenetrable close it in the air. If the board has stalled, sacrifice everything to Vampiric Rites and Plumb the Forbidden, draw six or seven, then Exsanguinate for the rest. Rogue's Passage is the alternate line for a single lethal trampler.
- **Key interactions**: (1) A creature must *have* haste or flash **at the moment it dies** — equip Power Sneakers *before* sacrificing. (2) Split second stops responses but not static effects or replacement effects; it will not get a creature through an opponent's Ward or protection. (3) Wake to Slaughter's second creature is **exiled**, not sacrificed, so Shadow does not draw off it. (4) Blasphemous Act is safe to point at your own board once Shadow is out — count the cards before you cast it.

## Why These Choices (Summary)
- **Haste is treated as a resource, not a keyword.** Rather than only playing creatures that already have haste, the deck runs five equipment, a repeatable combat-step granter, a one-mana cantrip that grants it, and a land that grants it — so the Shadow trigger applies to whatever body you actually need to cash in.
- **Free sacrifice outlets over efficient ones.** Woe Strider and Immersturm Predator cost nothing to activate, which is what lets you convert a creature into a card *in response* to removal rather than on your own turn. Vampiric Rites and Deadly Dispute are the paid outlets that also draw on their own.
- **Artifact mana is doubled up on purpose.** Seven rocks, an artifact land, and nine Treasure producers exist as much for Chaos Control as for ramp. Split second on a removal spell is close to unconditional in a duel.
- **One sweeper, twelve targeted answers.** Duel play rewards killing the specific problem; Blasphemous Act is kept as the single reset because it is nearly free and it is *synergistic* here — your creatures dying is the deck's engine.
- **Notable omissions**: no counterspells (not in Rakdos), no graveyard-hate beyond Bojuka Bog and Immersturm Predator, and no way to answer an opposing enchantment other than Chaos Warp and Feed the Swarm.

## Analysis

**Strengths**:
- **Card advantage that scales with aggression** — most aggro decks run out of gas; this one draws harder the more it attacks and trades, so chump-blocking it is actively bad for the opponent.
- **Resilient to removal** — killing a creature in response to a free sacrifice outlet just gives you the card anyway, and killing Shadow himself draws you a card.
- **Uncounterable at will** — split second off a single Treasure means combo and control opponents cannot interact with your key spells on the stack.
- **A very low curve with a real top end** — 31 cards at 2 CMC or less, but Smaug the Impenetrable and Goldspan Dragon end games that go long.

**Weaknesses/Missing Staples**:
- **Sweepers**: only Blasphemous Act. Against a go-wide token opponent you are relying on 1-for-1 removal and racing; Massacre Wurm, Chain Reaction and Decree of Pain are all in the collection if that matchup shows up.
- **Enchantment removal**: Chaos Warp and Feed the Swarm only. A resolved Rest in Peace or a pillowfort enchantment is close to game-over.
- **Lifegain / stabilisation**: essentially none. Vengeful Bloodwitch and Bastion of Remembrance trickle life, but the deck has no way to recover from a bad race.
- **Colour-intensive commander**: {B}{B}{R}{R} on turn four wants a near-perfect mana base. Nine dual-producing lands plus two fetches is good, but a two-Swamp opener still delays Shadow.
- **Determined Iteration is conditional**: it does nothing without a creature token on board. Four token producers support it, but it can sit dead.

## Next Steps (Optional Suggestions)
- **Test the sweeper count.** If the metagame is go-wide, swap **Reassembling Skeleton** for **Massacre Wurm** — it is one-sided and the collection has it.
- **Consider Rionya, Fire Dancer** ({3}{R}{R}) over Quaketusk Boar if you want more explosive burst; the token copies gain haste but are *exiled* rather than sacrificed, so they do not feed Shadow.
- **Consider Gratuitous Violence** ({2}{R}{R}{R}) as a finisher if games are going long — it doubles all your creature damage, but the {R}{R}{R} fights the commander's {B}{B}.
- **Cheap mana rocks** are the best upgrade slot: a Mind Stone or Talisman of Indulgence would both ramp and turn on Chaos Control a turn earlier than Pentad Prism does.
- If **Hoard Mentality** gets built in paper, Smaug the Impenetrable needs replacing here — **Lava Serpent** ({5}{R} 5/5 haste with cycling) is the closest available substitute.

## Suggested Deck Names

- **Chaos Control** — The commander's own ability name, and a literal description of what split second does to a game: the opponent's turn stops mattering. Punchy, two words, and instantly recognisable to anyone who has held a Sega controller.
- **Dead Fast** — The whole deck in two words: your creatures are fast, they die, and that is the point. Doubles as the aggro clock and the aristocrats engine.
- **Split Second Wind** — A pun on the Chaos Control keyword and the deck's refusal to run out of cards; every creature that dies hands you a second wind.
- **The Ultimate Lifeform** — Shadow's canon title, applied to a deck whose creatures come back from Zombify, Wake to Slaughter, Flamewake Phoenix and Reassembling Skeleton. Ominous, and only half a joke.
