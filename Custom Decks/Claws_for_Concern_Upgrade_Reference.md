## Do NOT buy

| Card | Why not |
|---|---|
| **Silver Bolt**, **Wolfhunter's Quiver** | They say "Werewolf" because they are Innistrad *villager* cards printed to **destroy** werewolves. Hate cards, not support. |
| **Cavern of Souls** (~€39) | Wolf and Werewolf are **separate creature types** and this deck is split ~26/8. Naming one leaves the other unprotected — €39 for a land that half-works. |
| **Coat of Arms** | Symmetric (pumps opposing tribal decks), and the type split means your Wolves and Werewolves count in separate buckets. |
| **Shared Animosity**, **Vanquisher's Banner**, **Icon of Ancestry** | The same split-tribe tax. A realistic attack of 4 Wolf tokens + 4 Werewolves shares **no** creature type, so Shared Animosity gives +3/+0, not +7/+0. |
| **Maskwood Nexus** | Anti-synergistic — makes everything a Human, breaking Immerwolf's transform lock and blanking Cult of the Waxing Moon. |
| **Garruk Relentless** | Colour identity **black-green** — illegal under Tovolar. |
| **Craterhoof Behemoth**, **End-Raze Forerunners** | You already run two "double the board each combat" effects. |
| Symmetric sweepers (**Blasphemous Act**, **Chandra's Ignition**) | You are reliably the widest board at the table. |

## The one thing money cannot fix

**Only four cards in all of Magic can make it night** — Tovolar, The Celestus, Unnatural Moonrise and Into the Night — and **you already own all four.** The deck's defining weakness, that the night plan barely functions without the commander, is unbuyable.

The closest partial answer is **Vivien, Champion of the Wilds (€0.50)** in Tier 3. The free route to night is casting *zero* spells on your own turn, which a 40-creature deck can never afford. Vivien lets you cast creatures as though they had flash, so you deploy on opponents' end steps and casting nothing on your turn stops being a sacrifice.
