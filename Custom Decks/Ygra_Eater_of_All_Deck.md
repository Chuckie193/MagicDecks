# Ygra, Eater of All — Golgari Food Aristocrats Commander

Commander: Ygra, Eater of All (3 generic, Black, Green — Black/Green)

## Overview
- **Strategy**: Ygra rewrites the battlefield so that *every* creature — yours and your opponents' — is a Food artifact. That turns the whole table into fuel: every creature that dies anywhere puts two +1/+1 counters on Ygra, and every "sacrifice a Food" or "sacrifice an artifact" cost in the deck can now eat any creature you control. Grind incremental drain through Vengeful Bloodwitch and Ninja Teen while Ygra balloons, then close with a trampling, Zopandrel-doubled commander swing.
- **Intended for**: Multiplayer pod
- **Card pool**: Avoid reserved decks (Full Deployment, Squirreled Away, Dance of the Elements)

## Lore

Ygra pads through the battlefield and every hero starts smelling faintly of *lunch*. Armies become appetisers. Champions become leftovers. Her pack serves each course gladly. Nobody leaves the table until she *cleans her plate*.

## Key Synergies
- **Ygra + any board wipe (The Last Ronin)**: Every creature on the table is a Food, so a wipe that kills ten creatures gives Ygra **twenty** +1/+1 counters. You are the only player who profits from resetting the board.
- **Ygra + Supper for Spiders**: Wipe the board, then reclaim every creature that died from your opponents' graveyards as Foods under your control. A two-mana instant that can rebuild your board and empty three graveyards at once.
- **Ygra + Technodrome / Thornvault Forager**: Their costs read "sacrifice another artifact" and "sacrifice a Food". Under Ygra, every creature you control satisfies both — so these become free-to-use sacrifice outlets that turn any creature into a card or two mana.
- **Ygra + Reassembling Skeleton + Vengeful Bloodwitch**: For {1}{B} per turn, return the Skeleton, sacrifice it, drain an opponent for 1 and put two counters on Ygra. A slow but completely inevitable engine that needs no other pieces.
- **Ygra + Zopandrel + Sword of Vengeance**: Zopandrel doubles Ygra's power at the beginning of combat and the Sword grants trample and haste. A 12/12 Ygra becomes a 24/24 trampler — lethal commander damage out of nowhere.
- **Ygra + Honored Dreyleader**: Dreyleader counts Squirrels *and Foods*, so under Ygra it enters with a counter for every creature you control and grows every time any creature enters.

## Short Mulligan and Play Notes
- **Early game (turns 1–4)**: Keep hands with two lands and a ramp piece. Llanowar Elves, Druid of the Cowl and Sol Ring all get Ygra out a turn early. Don't over-commit creatures before you have a sacrifice outlet — you want bodies *and* a way to convert them.
- **Mid game (turns 5–8)**: Land Ygra and immediately look for Reassembling Skeleton or a Food producer. Once Ygra is online, let the table fight: every creature that dies anywhere grows her. Hold Overprotect or Power Sneakers for the first removal spell aimed at her.
- **Late game**: Two routes. Grind them out with Vengeful Bloodwitch and Ninja Teen draining three opponents at once, or set up the combat kill — Sword of Vengeance for trample, Zopandrel to double her, and swing for 21+ commander damage.
- **Key interactions**: Remember that Ygra's ability applies to *opponents'* creatures too, so their creatures dying grows her and their creatures are legal targets for anything that says "target Food". Also remember her ward is "sacrifice a Food" — under her own ability, opponents can pay that by sacrificing any creature they control, so it is a speed bump, not real protection.

## Why These Choices (Summary)
- **Sacrifice outlets over raw power**: Ygra needs bodies to convert. Reassembling Skeleton, Tunnel Rats, Hungry Ghoul, Technodrome and Tom, Bert, and William are all cheap and repeatable, which matters more than any individual big creature.
- **Drain payoffs that scale with opponent count**: Vengeful Bloodwitch, Ninja Teen, Arnyn and the Vampire package all hit every opponent, so their value roughly triples in a pod relative to a duel.
- **Mana base built around a thin pool**: The reserved-deck filter left only 14 usable nonbasic lands in Golgari, so the deck runs 23 basics and four basic-fetchers (Escape Tunnel, Evolving Wilds, Terramorphic Expanse, Hobbit Hole) plus Rampant Growth and Old Thrush to compensate.
- **Two win routes**: Incremental drain that does not require combat, and a Zopandrel-doubled commander swing that can come from nowhere. Neither route is fragile to a single removal spell.

## Analysis

**Strengths**:
- Ygra is the rare commander who *profits* from other people's removal and board wipes — in a four-player pod she grows constantly without you spending a card.
- Ygra converts otherwise narrow cards into premium ones: "sacrifice an artifact" and "sacrifice a Food" costs become "sacrifice any creature", which is a real and unusual advantage.
- Two independent win conditions, one of which (drain) does not need combat at all.
- Very low curve (34 of 63 nonland cards cost 2 or less), so the deck functions on four or five lands.

**Weaknesses/Missing Staples**:
- **Board wipes — the big one**: The deck has exactly **one** true sweeper, The Last Ronin, against the 3–5 the pod guidance calls for. Decree of Pain, Plaguecrafter, Plumb the Forbidden and Bane of Progress are all physically sitting in **Squirreled Away**. This is a forced omission, not a card-quality judgement, and it is the single biggest thing separating this build from the full-collection version. Gnashing of Teeth is a partial, one-opponent substitute.
- **Food payoffs**: **Academy Manufactor** (Clue + Food + Treasure on every token trigger) is also locked in a reserved deck, as is Bastion of Remembrance. Their absence is why the deck leans on drain creatures rather than a dedicated Food engine.
- **Mana base**: 14 nonbasic lands is thin, and none of them are true untapped duals. Expect some awkward colour draws, particularly for the {B}{B} and {G}{G} costs.
- **Ygra's ward is soft**: Because she makes all creatures Food, opponents can pay her ward by sacrificing any creature they control. Treat her as unprotected and hold Power Sneakers or Overprotect accordingly.
- **No graveyard-order tutors**: The deck relies on drawing its engine pieces; there is no Entomb/Buried Alive effect in the eligible pool.

## Next Steps (Optional Suggestions)
- If you dismantle **Squirreled Away**, the three highest-impact additions are **Decree of Pain**, **Academy Manufactor** and **Plumb the Forbidden** — that alone would fix the sweeper problem and the Food-engine gap.
- Playtest whether 37 lands is right. With this curve you may be able to cut to 36 and add Send in the Pest or Deadly Plot.
- Consider whether Down, Down to Goblin-town earns its slot; it is the slowest card here and could become a second sweeper if one becomes available.
- If the pod is heavy on artifacts, Bumbleflower's Sharepot and Thrashing Brontodon can be joined by more artifact hate from the pool.
