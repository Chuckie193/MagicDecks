# Thorin Oakenshield — The Hobbit Story Commander

Commander: Thorin Oakenshield (Red, White — 2 CMC, Boros)

Overview
- Strategy: A retelling of *The Hobbit* in 100 cards, built around Thorin's **Storied** ability — control three or more artifacts, legendaries and/or Sagas and you have an *enduring story* for the rest of the game, giving all your artifacts and creatures ward 1. The deck is stuffed with legendary dwarves, quest artifacts and Sagas, so Storied switches on early and stays on. From there it plays as a wide, resilient Boros equipment deck: assemble the Company, arm them, and swing.
- Intended for: Both (single opponent and multiplayer pod)

## Lore

Thirteen dwarves, one burglar, and a mountain of stolen gold. Thorin marches east, and every step writes another verse. The dragon sleeps atop his birthright — but *songs have a way of waking things.*

Key Synergies
- **Thorin Oakenshield + the artifact lands (Ancient Den / Great Furnace / Rustvale Bridge)**: Storied counts artifacts, and these are artifacts that also produce mana — three of your Storied enablers cost you no card slots and dodge creature wipes entirely.
- **Fíli the Pathfinder + every nontoken Dwarf**: Each legendary dwarf you cast makes a free 2/2 Dwarf token, and Fíli's Storied mode gives the whole growing board +1/+1.
- **Belladonna Took + Fíli / recruit / Treasures**: Tokens arrive constantly from Dwarf tokens, Soldier tokens and Treasures — the third one each turn puts a +1/+1 counter on your entire team.
- **Skullclamp + Dwarf and Soldier tokens**: Clamping a 1/1 Soldier draws two cards immediately; with Fíli making 2/2 Dwarves you get a repeatable draw engine that also triggers Belladonna Took.
- **Dáin Ironfoot + Hammer of Nazahn / Thorin, Mountain-king**: Mass-attach your Equipment, then give every equipped attacker double strike — Thorin, Mountain-king also converts that attachment into direct damage.
- **The Eagles Are Coming! (kicked) + a board wipe**: Return your whole team to hand in response to a sweeper and get them all back as 4/4 flying Bird Soldiers next upkeep.

Short mulligan and play notes
- Early game: Keep hands with two or three lands and cheap plays. Thorin costs only RW, so he can land on turn two. Prioritise getting three artifacts/legendaries/Sagas out — a Signet, an artifact land and any legendary dwarf will do it, and once Storied is on it never turns off.
- Mid game: Deploy legendary dwarves to trigger Fíli, arm the biggest body with Equipment, and use Nori or Dáin Ironfoot to make combat maths miserable. Skullclamp turns spare tokens into fuel.
- Late game: Swing wide with a Fíli-pumped team under ward 1, or go tall — mass-attach with Thorin, Mountain-king or the Hammer and push through with trample and double strike. Smaug and the Misty Mountains Cold Dragon provide evasive finishers.
- Key interactions: Hold The Eagles Are Coming! as wipe insurance rather than casting it for value. Remember Blasphemous Act gets cheaper as boards grow — including your own, so it's a reset, not a finisher. Ward 1 from Thorin taxes every removal spell aimed at your team, so opponents will target Thorin first; Equipment like the Hammer's indestructibility helps protect him.

Why these choices (summary)
- The commander was chosen for mechanical fit as much as flavour: Storied literally counts artifacts, legendaries and Sagas, and a story-themed Hobbit deck naturally plays all three, so the ability is close to always-on.
- 48 of the 84 named cards come from the Hobbit set itself, so the deck reads as a retelling rather than a Boros pile with a theme pasted on — the map, Sting, the Black Arrow, the thrush, the barrel escape, the trolls at dawn, Smaug and the Battle of Five Armies all appear.
- Ramp is artifact-based, which double-dips as Storied enablers; three artifact lands do the same job for free.
- Sweepers are kept to two because the deck goes wide and doesn't want to undo its own board; targeted removal is prioritised instead, with The Eagles Are Coming! as a way to dodge opposing wipes.

Analysis
**Strengths**:
- Storied comes online fast and gives the whole board ward 1, which blanks a surprising amount of cheap interaction.
- Very strong token-and-Equipment engine: Fíli plus any dwarf produces bodies, Skullclamp converts them to cards, Belladonna Took rewards the volume.
- Low curve with a two-mana commander means fast starts, and the deck functions without drawing Thorin.

**Weaknesses/Missing Staples**:
- Board wipes: only two, and both are symmetrical. A go-wide deck genuinely doesn't want more, but it means a bigger opposing board can be hard to reset without losing your own.
- Card draw: outside Skullclamp and looting effects, raw draw is thin — Boros's usual weakness. Nothing in the collection fixes this cleanly in R/W.
- Colour identity cost: Red/White locks out Bilbo, Gandalf's blue version, Gollum, Beorn, Thranduil and the Mirkwood chapters. Roughly half the Hobbit story lives outside this deck's colours, and no Hobbit-set commander in the collection is wider than two colours.
- No graveyard recursion for creatures beyond The Mountain-king's Return, so a heavy sweeper can be hard to rebuild from.

Next steps (optional suggestions)
- If you ever pick up Tom Bombadil plus a real Saga package, the five-colour Saga build is the version of this idea that covers every chapter of the book — worth revisiting then.
- A companion Black/Green deck under Tom, Bert, and William would cover the wilderness chapters this one misses (trolls, Gollum, wargs, Beorn, Mirkwood), so the two decks together tell the whole story.
- If you want more raw power, additional Equipment tutors and a couple of protection pieces for Thorin would make the Storied plan more consistent.

## Suggested Deck Names

- **Unexpected Party** — The book's first chapter, and a fair description of what happens when Fíli starts spawning dwarves and your opponents suddenly face a full board.
- **Storied Company** — A triple meaning: Thorin's Company, the Storied keyword the deck is built on, and a company with a storied history.
- **There and Back Again** — Bilbo's own title for the tale; also nods to the Equipment shuttling back and forth and the Eagles bouncing your team home and back.
- **Erebor Ever After** — A pun on "ever after" — the fairy-tale ending waiting at the Lonely Mountain, assuming the dragon cooperates.
